================================================================
RADIO REALISM
================================================================

NUMBERS — every digit is spoken as a SEPARATE word. No exceptions inside
an utterance:
- altitudes: "one two thousand", "three thousand five hundred", "flight level
  three five zero"
- headings: "heading two seven zero" — never "two-seventy"
- frequencies: "one one eight decimal three" — always "decimal", never "point"
- squawk: "squawk four seven zero one" — never "forty-seven oh one"
- runways: "runway two seven left", "runway zero six"
   ALWAYS digit-by-digit. NEVER "runway eighteen", "runway twenty-two right",
   "runway thirty" — say "runway one eight", "runway two two right",
   "runway three zero".
- winds: "wind two five zero at one zero" — never "at ten", never "at thirty"
- visibility: spoken as a SINGLE WHOLE NUMBER of statute miles. NEVER
  digit-by-digit (because vis is always a single value, not a multi-digit
  string). Examples:
    7 sm    →  "visibility seven"
    10 sm   →  "visibility one zero"  (or simply "visibility ten" colloquially)
    1.5 sm  →  "visibility one and one half"  (fractional)
    0.5 sm  →  "visibility one half"
    0.25 sm →  "visibility one quarter" (or "RVR <feet>" in low IMC)
    5.8 sm  →  "visibility five" (round) — NEVER "visibility five eight"
  NEVER say "visibility two three" / "visibility five eight" / "visibility
  zero seven nine" — those read as multi-digit numbers (23, 58, 079) which
  is nonsense for visibility.
- aircraft model numbers when spoken: "seven three seven", "triple seven",
  "Airbus three two zero"

ALTIMETER — this is the most-violated rule. Altimeter is spoken as FOUR
DIGITS, NO "decimal" or "point" between them:
   29.92  →  "altimeter two niner niner two"  (or "two nine nine two")
   30.05  →  "altimeter three zero zero five"
   29.84  →  "altimeter two niner eight four"
NEVER "altimeter two nine decimal nine two" or "altimeter three zero point
zero five". The word "decimal" is ONLY for frequencies — not altimeter,
not visibility, not anything else.

CEILING — spoken as the number followed by "hundred", or as
"<thousands> thousand <hundreds> hundred":
   400 ft   →  "ceiling four hundred"
   1500 ft  →  "ceiling one thousand five hundred"
   8600 ft  →  "ceiling eight thousand six hundred"
NEVER spoken digit-by-digit ("ceiling four zero zero", "ceiling eight six
seven four"). Round to the nearest 100 ft when speaking.

The digit nine may be spoken as "niner" (an FAA convention to avoid confusion
with German "nein") — this is the ONLY allowed variant.
Bare digits and compound number words (twenty, thirty, forty, fifty, sixty,
seventy, eighty, ninety, ten, point) are FORBIDDEN inside the spoken text
(except "hundred" and "thousand" which are required for altitude/ceiling).
The speaker tag (left of the colon) may contain digits; the utterance must not.

CALLSIGNS — every aircraft has two forms:
- the SPEAKER TAG (left of the colon) is the ICAO callsign exactly as given in
  the briefing's "ICAO=" field for that aircraft.
- the SPOKEN form (inside the utterance) is the radio call name from the
  briefing's "spoken=" field for that aircraft, copied verbatim.
After first contact, the controller may abbreviate to the last three digits of
the spoken form. The pilot usually keeps the full form.

HEAVY / SUPER SUFFIX — the wake-category rule that is violated more often
than any other in synthetic ATC data. If the briefing marks an aircraft as
wake "heavy", the spoken callsign MUST be followed by the word "heavy" EVERY
single time it is spoken, by both controller and pilot, in EVERY phase
including ramp and ground. Same for wake "super". L and M wake → no suffix.

CRITICAL: wake=L and wake=M aircraft NEVER receive any spoken suffix. Never
say "medium", "light", or anything else after the spoken callsign for L/M
aircraft. ONLY "heavy" (for wake=H) and "super" (for wake=J) are spoken
suffixes. If the briefing says wake=M, the callsign is just the spoken form
— "Southwest one two three four", not "Southwest one two three four medium".
  WRONG (controller, focal is wake "heavy"):
    "<spoken-callsign>, Tower, cleared for takeoff"
  RIGHT:
    "<spoken-callsign> heavy, Tower, cleared for takeoff"
  WRONG (pilot readback, focal is wake "heavy"):
    "Cleared for takeoff, <spoken-callsign>"
  RIGHT:
    "Cleared for takeoff, <spoken-callsign> heavy"
Re-read the briefing's wake column for each aircraft before you write the
first turn, and check every spoken callsign as you write.

FACILITY NAMES AND SPEAKER TAGS — use ONLY the spoken airport name from the
briefing for in-utterance facility references, and use ONLY the speaker tags
listed in the briefing's "Facility speaker tags" line. Do not substitute a
city name from anywhere else; do not invent tags like `<city>_TWR` when the
briefing already specifies `<ICAO>_TWR`.

NEVER speak the ICAO airport code as part of the facility identifier. The
ICAO code (e.g. KSFO, KCLT, KORD, KBUF) is for the SPEAKER TAG only — never
in the spoken text. Always use the briefing's "Airport spoken name" field:
  WRONG: "KSFO Tower, United one two three four"
  RIGHT: "San Francisco Tower, United one two three four"
  WRONG: "KBUF Center, Hawker two three"  (and KBUF isn't a Center anyway)
  RIGHT: "Buffalo Approach, Hawker two three"
Same for pilots addressing the facility — use the spoken name, never the ICAO.

CENTER FACILITY NAMING — Centers are NOT named for airports. When phase=
center, the briefing supplies an "ARTCC" line giving the exact spoken name
(e.g. "Oakland Center", "Atlanta Center"). Use that name verbatim when the
controller self-identifies. Do NOT substitute the airport's city name, the
ICAO, or any nearby town — the briefing already chose the correct ARTCC.

  WRONG: Pleasanton Center, November three zero four zero eight, ident.
  RIGHT: Oakland Center, November three zero four zero eight, ident.

  WRONG: Iowa County Center, climb and maintain one one thousand.
  RIGHT: Chicago Center, climb and maintain one one thousand.

  WRONG: San Francisco Center, contact Approach one two zero decimal one.
  RIGHT: Oakland Center, contact Approach one two zero decimal one.

The only spoken ARTCC name you may use in a center scenario is the one in
the briefing's "ARTCC" line.

ROLE DISCIPLINE — strict. Each facility ONLY issues what its role owns; any
other clearance type is a violation. The model often blurs these — be strict.

  RAMP — pushback approval, gate movement, ramp-area instructions only.
    RAMP MAY: "pushback approved [direction]", "hold position", "contact ground".
    RAMP MUST NOT: issue squawks, runway taxi clearance, hold-short of runway,
      taxiway routing past the ramp boundary, runway crossings, line-up-and-wait,
      takeoff clearance, landing clearance, vectors, altitude assignments.
    Once pushback complete, hand off to GROUND.

  GROUND — taxi routing, hold-short, squawk assignment, handoff to TOWER.
    GROUND MAY: "taxi to runway X via [taxiways]", "hold short of [runway]",
      "squawk XXXX", "altimeter XXXX", "contact tower", "monitor tower".
    GROUND MUST NOT: issue "line up and wait", "cleared for takeoff",
      "cleared for landing", en-route descent/climb instructions, vectors,
      pushback approval (that's ramp).

  TOWER — line-up-and-wait, takeoff clearance, landing clearance, initial
    departure heading (one or two instructions max), handoff to DEPARTURE
    after departure or to GROUND after landing.
    TOWER MAY: "line up and wait runway X", "cleared for takeoff runway X
      [+ heading]", "cleared to land runway X", "go around", "fly heading
      [direction] climb [altitude up to ~5000ft]".
    TOWER MUST NOT: issue full taxi-to-gate instructions (handoff to ground),
      en-route altitude assignments (e.g. flight levels), ILS clearance
      (that's approach), full pushback (that's ramp), squawks pre-departure
      (that's ground).

  APPROACH — vectors, altitude assignments, ILS clearance, handoff to TOWER.
    APPROACH MAY: "turn left/right heading", "descend and maintain X",
      "cleared ILS runway X approach", "contact tower [freq]",
      "expect [approach/runway]".
    APPROACH MUST NOT: issue landing clearance (that's tower), pushback or
      taxi (those are ramp/ground).

  CENTER — en-route vectors and altitudes, handoffs to APPROACH or another
    CENTER sector or TOWER (small airport direct).
    CENTER MAY: "climb/descend and maintain [altitude]", "turn left/right
      heading", "fly direct [waypoint]", "contact [next facility]".
    CENTER MUST NOT: issue ILS approach clearance (that's approach), landing
      clearance, takeoff clearance, taxi instructions, pushback.

Plausible handoff destinations:
- GROUND → TOWER or CLEARANCE
- TOWER → DEPARTURE (outbound) or GROUND (after landing)
- APPROACH → TOWER (when established for landing) or CENTER (after missed)
- CENTER → APPROACH, TOWER, or another CENTER sector
- RAMP → GROUND

FORBIDDEN PHRASES — these are not real US ATC phraseology. Never use ANY
of these or close variants:
- ANY "frequency check" variant: "frequency check complete/required/
  recommended/your receiver/altimeter/authorized/not required/is checkered",
  "frequencies changing", "frequency change required" — there is no real
  ATC phrase that contains the words "frequency check". Just hand off.
- "QNH" (US uses "altimeter")
- "good luck", "good night" (controllers do not use these; "good day" is OK)
- "have a nice landing", "have a smooth flight", "have a great day"
- "out" as a sign-off (military RT, not US civilian ATC)
- "track stable", "visual descent", "descend gradient", "expect approach
  climb gradient", "sequential N knots"
- "identify yourself on contact" (pilots check in by callsign)
- "tower is ready when you are", "tower is with you"
- "with speed", "with your gear down", "with information" (when not asking
  for ATIS)
- "position and hold" (this is the OLD pre-2010 phraseology; use "line up
  and wait")
- "report weather" from controller to pilot (pilots may give PIREPs, but
  controllers don't ask for general weather reports)
- "at your own discretion" for runway crossings
- "frequency is checkered", "fire suppression guidance en route"

Standard handoff is just "contact <facility> <freq>" — no padding, no filler.

PLAINTEXT ONLY — the output must be plain text, one turn per line, NO
markdown formatting in spoken utterances. NO **bold**, NO _italic_, NO
ALL-CAPS words for emphasis, NO em-dashes inside speech, NO literal digit
characters in spoken text. The text following the colon is the literal
transcript of what would be heard on the radio.

BREVITY AND CADENCE:
- Each turn is one to three short sentences.
- One instruction per transmission when possible. Stack at most two or three
  data items.
- Pilots read back ONLY safety-of-flight items: assigned altitude, heading,
  runway, squawk, frequency, hold-short, line-up-and-wait, takeoff/landing
  clearance. Pilots do NOT read back wind, visibility, ceiling, altimeter
  setting, advisory traffic, or general information — acknowledge briefly
  ("roger", "copy", or just the callsign) or not at all.
  WRONG: "Wind two zero four at nine, visibility seven, altimeter two nine
          eight three, <spoken-callsign>."
  RIGHT: "Roger, <spoken-callsign>."
- Light disfluencies are welcome but not required: "uh", "stand by", a
  repeated callsign, a partial readback corrected on the next turn.

================================================================
SCENARIO FAITHFULNESS
================================================================

Read the briefing carefully and use its values verbatim:
- The assigned SQUAWK code exactly as given. The sampler already excludes
  reserved codes (7500 hijack, 7600 lost-comm, 7700 emergency) — do NOT
  substitute one in. If you ever find yourself writing "squawk seven five
  zero zero", "squawk seven six zero zero", or "squawk seven seven zero
  zero", that is wrong: use the briefing's squawk verbatim.
- The focal RUNWAY exactly as given, including any side letter (L / R / C
  spoken as "left" / "right" / "center"). Do not silently change sides.
- The initial FREQUENCY is implicit (you are already on it); any handoff
  frequency you invent must be plausible for the receiving facility.
- The SID/STAR field: if it says "none", you MUST NOT mention any SID or STAR
  name. If it gives a name, see the WAYPOINT PRONUNCIATION section below.
- The wake category for EVERY aircraft.
- Use ONLY the callsigns listed in the briefing. Do not invent additional
  aircraft. The focal aircraft (marked [FOCAL]) MUST appear as a speaker.

The briefing lists every aircraft on this frequency. For light traffic the
exchange may be focal-only; for moderate or heavy traffic, weave in 1-3
short routine transmissions to or from background aircraft.

================================================================
WAYPOINT PRONUNCIATION
================================================================

This rule applies to EVERY waypoint mention — SID/STAR names from the
briefing's SID/STAR field, direct-to fixes, intersection clearances,
transition fixes, arrival fixes. Anywhere a 5-letter aviation code appears.

CORE PRINCIPLE: FAA designs every waypoint code to be PRONOUNCEABLE AS AN
ENGLISH WORD (or near-word). Letter-spelling is a LAST RESORT. Real
controllers find a word to say in 95%+ of cases.

Find the closest-sounding English word the letters evoke and say THAT word.
Common transformation patterns:

  DROP A TRAILING VOWEL:
    EBAYE → "Ebay"      (drop final E — the auction-site pun)
    ZEPPA → "Zepp"      (drop final A — Led Zeppelin)
    DRAKO → "Drake"     (drop final O — singer / lake)
    JOSHU → "Joshua"    (add trailing A back)
    PARKE → "Parker"    (add trailing R back)

  DROP/INSERT INTERNAL VOWELS:
    BDEGA → "Bodega"    (insert O after B)
    GOLDN → "Golden"    (insert E before N)
    SUPRR → "Supper"    (insert E, soften RR)
    PIGLT → "Piglet"    (insert E before T)
    SADDE → "Saddle"    (insert L before E)
    ROBER → "Robber"    (double the B)
    NIITE → "Night"     (double-I → long-I sound)

  PHONETIC EQUIVALENTS / ALT SPELLINGS:
    BOACH → "Beach"     (silent A; same sound as BEACH)
    WAVEY → "Wavy"      (drop silent E)
    DAWGS → "Dogs"      (slang spelling)
    HONIE → "Honey"
    LUCKI → "Lucky"
    TRUKN → "Truckin"   (drop G; rocker's "truckin'")

  CLEAR PUNS / NAMES / PLACES:
    PIRAT → "Pirate"
    RIVET → "Rivet"
    SERFR → "Surfer"
    BRUNS → "Bruins"    (Boston Bruins sports pun)
    BAYST → "Beast"     (A→E vowel swap; AY → long E)
    MUNCI → "Muncie"    (Indiana city)
    ORLDO → "Orlando"

  VOWEL SWAPS — FAA uses similar-sounding substitutions:
    A ↔ E: BAYST → "Beast", JAYDE → "Jade"
    A ↔ O: DUMBA → "Dumbo", CASBA → "Casbah"
    I ↔ Y: BYRDS → "Birds", HYANN → "Hyannis"

  ABBREVIATIONS OF WORDS:
    OFFSH → "Offshore"
    EAGUL → "Eagle"
    CHANL → "Channel"
    LIINS → "Lions"

NATO PHONETIC SPELLING — use only when:
- The letters are clearly random consonants with NO vowel pattern
  (KRPTC, PQRWZ, ZQRWY, XKJPR, FGHJP, BCDFG) — always NATO here.
- OR you've tried the transformation patterns and no word emerges. Real
  controllers letter-spell unfamiliar waypoints too — this is legitimate
  ATC behavior, not a failure.

For NATO, spell each letter with the alphabet, followed by the digit if it's
a SID/STAR name:
  XKJPR3 → "X-ray Kilo Juliet Papa Romeo three departure"
  PQRWZ  → "Papa Quebec Romeo Whiskey Zulu" (direct-to clearance)

REASONING APPROACH (apply mentally before answering):
  1. Look at the letter sequence as a whole.
  2. Ask: "If I said these letters as if they spelled an English word, what
     would they sound like?"
  3. Try the patterns above: drop trailing vowel, insert missing vowel,
     phonetic equivalent, recognize as a name/place, vowel swap.
  4. ONLY if no transformation yields a plausible English word, fall back
     to NATO.

CONSISTENCY — once you decide on a pronunciation for a waypoint in a
transcript, use the SAME pronunciation in every subsequent mention (both
controller and pilot readback).

For SID/STAR specifically: the trailing digit is always spoken individually
("Bodega three departure", "Wavy eight departure").

================================================================
EVENT LENGTH AND CLOSURE
================================================================

- routine: 4-8 turns
- abnormals (go_around, hold, runway_change, weather_deviation, etc.): 6-15 turns
- emergencies (emergency_*): 12-30 turns

Run the exchange to a natural close — either a frequency handoff appropriate
to the phase, immediately followed by the focal aircraft's readback of the
new frequency and callsign; or a logical stopping point such as a
cleared-to-land readback or sign-off after handoff. Do not cut off
mid-clearance or mid-readback.

================================================================
OUTPUT FORMAT
================================================================

Plaintext only. One turn per line:

    SPEAKER: utterance

No JSON, no markdown, no code fences, no headings, no commentary. The focal
aircraft's callsign must appear as a speaker.
